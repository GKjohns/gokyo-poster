# New Analogy Concepts (Sprint B)

For the 10 techniques where Kyle asked for a different scene, plus one fallback concept
for Tsurikomi Goshi in case the bucket keeps pouring the wrong way.

House rules applied to every concept below: mechanically faithful, one drawable frozen
frame, explainable to a child, whimsical enough to clear gpt-image-2 safety, **no broom
or any sweeping tool as the agent** (rejected three times in review), and no reuse of a
scene already on the poster.

Scenes already spoken for (checked against all 40): skateboard, bicycle-over-the-curb,
classroom trip, revolving door, wheelbarrow, shepherd's crook, piggyback, banana peel,
mopped floor, playground high-bar, well bucket, shore wave, dog and leash and bench,
parking barrier, mud boot, fishing boat rail, pool edge, chain fence, seesaw, marionette,
airplane game (twice), grain sack, rolling pin, snow shovel, trampoline, turnstile,
tetherball, bull and cape, garden fence, garden hose, folding table, barrel, rodeo bull,
catapult, tipping chair, yanked rug.

## 1. Osoto Gari — *firm replacement* (`data/group-1.json`)

**Mechanic in one line:** the weight is rocked back onto one heel, then that single
supporting leg is reaped out **backward** from behind, so leg and body travel the same
way and the whole person lands flat on their back.

**Candidates**

| # | Concept | Why it maps (one line) |
|---|---|---|
| a | **Slide tackle from behind** — a kid leaning back to head a ball, a friend slides in and hooks the standing calf backward | The one supporting leg is carried backward in the same direction the body is already falling: the literal reap. |
| b | **Runaway sled** — one boot planted on a sled parked on a hill; the sled slides off downhill behind you and takes the boot with it | The support under the committed heel is carried away backward, in the direction of the lean, so the body follows it down. |
| c | **Kicked-out kickstand** — a loaded bicycle propped back on its stand, a passing sneaker folds the stand backward | The single rear support is swept out the same way the load is leaning. |

**Ranking.** (a) first: it is the only one where the thing removed is a *leg*, the contact
is behind the calf, and the sweep direction is unmistakable, and a slide tackle is the one
real-life situation everybody has actually seen do this (which was Kyle's whole complaint
about the broom). (b) second: clean and funny, but it sits close to Deashi Harai's
skateboard on the poster, distinguished mainly by direction. (c) last: from a side view a
bicycle on a kickstand falls *sideways*, not backward, so the frame would lie about the
mechanic, and Hiza Guruma already owns the bicycle.

### 1a. `slide-tackle` — TOP PICK

- **analogy_name:** `Taken out by the slide tackle`
- **analogy:** `You lean back to head a high ball, your whole weight settled on one back heel. A friend slides in from behind and their leg hooks that standing leg and carries it away backward. Your foot goes up, your shoulders go down, and you land flat on your back in the grass, still grinning.`
- **why_it_maps:** `The weight is committed behind the heel first, and then the single supporting leg is reaped backward in the same direction as the fall. That order, and that direction, is osoto gari.`
- **image_prompt:**

```
Side view of a grassy park pitch, one frozen instant, whimsical cartoon ink drawing:
a smiling child stands leaning far BACKWARD with both arms up and chin tilted to the
sky, all their weight settled on one back heel; a second smiling child has slid in low
from BEHIND them along the grass, one leg stretched straight out so that the back of
the slider's calf is hooked against the BACK of the standing child's supporting calf
and is sweeping that leg BACKWARD and UPWARD, away from the direction the leaning
child is facing; the swept foot is already knee-high off the grass and still rising
backward, and the leaning child's shoulders are dropping BACKWARD and DOWN toward the
grass in that same backward direction, body flat as a felled plank, arms windmilling,
one shoe flying off; the single point where the slider's calf meets the back of the
standing calf is the only contact between them and is the point the whole fall turns
on; a soccer ball bounces away and dandelion fluff scatters; both faces are laughing,
comic cartoon peril, nobody hurt; low side-on viewpoint at knee height so that the
swept leg and the falling torso plainly travel the same way, backward.
```

### 1b. `runaway-sled` — SECOND

- **analogy_name:** `The sled leaves without you`
- **analogy:** `You plant one boot on a sled parked at the top of a hill and lean back to shout at your friends. The sled sets off downhill behind you and takes that boot with it. Your foot goes one way, backward and up, and the rest of you goes down flat in the snow.`
- **why_it_maps:** `Your weight is already committed backward over one foot, and then that foot is carried away backward under you, which is exactly the direction osoto gari reaps the supporting leg.`
- **image_prompt:**

```
Side view of a snowy hillside, one frozen instant, whimsical cartoon ink drawing:
a laughing child faces uphill and leans far BACKWARD, one boot planted flat on a
wooden sled behind them and the other foot already lifted off the snow; the sled has
started sliding DOWNHILL and BACKWARD, away behind the child, carrying that planted
boot with it so the leg is dragged backward and upward and is now higher than the
child's hips; the child's shoulders and head are dropping BACKWARD and DOWN in the
same downhill direction, back about to slap flat onto the snow, arms windmilling,
scarf and knitted hat flying; the boot never leaves the sled, and the sled and the
falling body travel the same way, backward down the slope; loose powder sprays
backward along the sled's path; comic cartoon peril, delighted face, nobody hurt;
low side-on view so the escaping sled and the flat backward fall read in one line.
```

## 2. Harai Goshi — *firm replacement* (`data/group-2.json`)

**Mechanic in one line:** the chest is drawn forward and committed over a pivot at hip
height while the legs are swept **up and backward** from behind, so the body rotates
right over the pivot and lands beyond it.

**Candidates**

| # | Concept | Why it maps (one line) |
|---|---|---|
| a | **Leapfrog partner stands up** — mid-vault, the crouching friend straightens and lifts your thighs skyward | The friend's back is the hip-height pivot and their rise is the sweep that lifts the legs, so you wheel over the top. |
| b | **Spotter's boost over the gym vault** — hips parked on the padded box, the coach sweeps a forearm under both thighs | Padded box at hip height plus a lift under the thighs from behind equals a full rotation over the box. |
| c | **Wave over the sea wall** — bent over a low sea wall when a wave lifts your legs from behind | Fixed pivot at the waist plus a lift under the legs, but the poster is already full of rails and walls. |

**Ranking.** (a) first: Kyle's own hint was *something else could be lifting their body up*,
and in leapfrog the pivot and the lifter are the same friendly body, so a child reads the
whole mechanic in one glance without a single implausible object. (b) second: equally
faithful, adds a distinct setting, and the coach's forearm under the thighs is exactly the
sweeping leg. (c) last: another horizontal rail, and the poster has too many already.

### 2a. `leapfrog` — TOP PICK

- **analogy_name:** `Leapfrog partner stands up`
- **analogy:** `You launch into a leapfrog vault with both hands on your friend's crouched back. Halfway over, your friend decides to stand up. Their back lifts your middle, their shoulders take your thighs skyward, and instead of clearing them you wheel right over the top and land on the far side.`
- **why_it_maps:** `The crouched back is tori's hip, a fixed pivot at your waist, and the sudden rise is the sweeping leg lifting your thighs from behind, so a committed upper body rotates all the way over.`
- **image_prompt:**

```
Side view of a grassy schoolyard, one frozen instant, whimsical cartoon ink drawing:
a laughing child is folded over the back of a second crouching child in a leapfrog
vault, the vaulter's beltline resting square on top of the crouching child's back so
that back is the pivot at hip height; the crouching child is grinning and RISING,
knees straightening and their back driving UPWARD, which throws the vaulter's thighs
UP and BACKWARD toward the sky so both of the vaulter's legs are already above head
height with heels pointing at the clouds; at the same time the vaulter's head and
shoulders are committed DOWNWARD on the far side of the crouching child, hands
reaching for the grass beyond, hair hanging straight down; the whole vaulter is caught
at the top of one continuous forward rotation around the crouching back as an axle,
legs up on the near side, head down on the far side; a cap and an apple tumble loose;
both faces are delighted, comic cartoon peril, nobody hurt; side-on composition at hip
height so the crouching back reads clearly as the fulcrum of the rotation.
```

### 2b. `vault-boost` — SECOND

- **analogy_name:** `The spotter's helpful boost`
- **analogy:** `You run at the gym vault, get your hips onto the padded top, and the coach gives your thighs a boost from behind. A little too much boost: your legs go up over your head, your middle stays parked on the pad, and you roll straight over the far side onto the mat.`
- **why_it_maps:** `The padded box at hip height is tori's hip holding your middle in place, and the forearm sweeping up under both thighs is the sweeping leg, so the body wheels over the pivot.`
- **image_prompt:**

```
Side view of a school gymnasium, one frozen instant, whimsical cartoon ink drawing:

pivot; a grinning coach stands BEHIND the box and has swept one forearm UNDER both of
```

## 3. Harai Tsurikomi Ashi — *firm replacement* (`data/group-3.json`)

**Mechanic in one line:** you are held up from above so your feet carry almost no weight,
and the advancing foot is swept **sideways** before it can land and bite.

**Candidates**

| # | Concept | Why it maps (one line) |
|---|---|---|
| a | **Ski hill rope tow** — the rope hauls you up and forward while both skis skid sideways out from under you | The rope is literally the lift-and-draw, and with the weight off the feet a single sideways slide takes them away. |
| b | **First time on the ice** — a friend tows you by both hands and your blades slide out sideways together | Hands held up front take the load off the feet, so the smallest sideways slip removes both of them at once. |
| c | **Held by the hood on wet tile** — a parent holding a kid's hood while their sneakers skid sideways | Same support-from-above, but the hood grip is a weaker, less legible version of the lifting pull. |

**Ranking.** (a) first: a rope tow *is* tsurikomi — an upward-and-forward pull on a line —
so the lift half of the technique is visible as an object, not implied, and skis make the
sideways escape of the feet unmistakable. (b) second: warmer and more universal, and the
hands-held-high support reads instantly. (c) last: the grip is small and the direction of
the lift is vague.

### 3a. `rope-tow` — TOP PICK

- **analogy_name:** `Hanging off the rope tow`
- **analogy:** `You grab the ski hill's rope tow and it hauls you up and forward with half your weight hanging off your arms. Right then one ski slides sideways. With the rope still holding you up there is nothing to catch: both skis shoot off to the side and you swing there like a bell.`
- **why_it_maps:** `The rope pulling up and forward is the tsurikomi lift that makes your feet light, and with almost no weight on them one sideways sweep takes both feet away before they can take the load.`
- **image_prompt:**

```
Side view of a snowy beginner ski slope, one frozen instant, whimsical cartoon ink
drawing: a laughing skier grips a moving tow rope with both hands high above and in
front of them, the rope stretched taut and pulling their body UP and FORWARD up the
hill so the arms are stretched straight and the weight visibly hangs from the rope;
below them both skis have shot SIDEWAYS out from under the body together, skimming
away horizontally across the slope to one side in a plume of powder, legs trailing
after the skis and both boots now well outside the line of the body with nothing at
all underneath the hips; the taut rope and the two gripping hands overhead are the
only support and the point the whole body swings from, torso tilting under it; a
knitted hat and one mitten fly loose and the snow spray blows in the same sideways
direction as the escaping skis; comic cartoon peril, big grin, nobody hurt; low
side-on view showing both the upward pull of the rope and the sideways escape of the
feet in one frame.
```

### 3b. `ice-hands` — SECOND

- **analogy_name:** `First time on the ice`
- **analogy:** `A friend skates backward holding both your hands, towing you along so half your weight is on their grip. Your blades barely touch the ice, and then they slide out sideways all at once. You end up hanging off two hands with your feet somewhere off to the left.`
- **why_it_maps:** `Being towed by the hands lifts the load off your feet, exactly like the lifting pull, so a small sideways slide sends both feet away before either can take weight.`
- **image_prompt:**

```
Side view of an ice rink, one frozen instant, whimsical cartoon ink drawing: a
grinning skater glides backward holding both hands of a wobbly beginner, arms
stretched and pulling the beginner UP and FORWARD so the beginner's weight plainly
hangs from the joined hands, shoulders hoisted and body leaning; the beginner's two
skates have slid SIDEWAYS out from under them together, both blades skating away
horizontally to one side across the ice with shaved ice spraying in that same sideways
direction, the legs trailing after the blades so both feet are now far outside the
line of the body and nothing is under the hips; the joined hands out in front and
above are the only support and the pivot the beginner's body swings from, a long scarf
streaming; small skaters in the distance; comic cartoon peril, both faces laughing,
nobody hurt; side-on composition showing the upward pull at the hands and the sideways
escape at the feet at the same instant.
```

## 4. Hane Goshi — *firm replacement* (`data/group-3.json`)

**Mechanic in one line:** a coiled, bent leg loaded across the thighs snaps straight and
**bounces** the body up off it, and the body then rotates over the top.

**Candidates**

| # | Concept | Why it maps (one line) |
|---|---|---|
| a | **Bent sapling springs upright** — sitting astride a young tree bent to the ground when it straightens | The trunk under the thighs is the coiled leg and its snap upward launches the rider up and over in a somersault. |
| b | **Playground spring rider** — the big coil under the seat squashes flat, then fires | A spring directly under the seat releases and pops the rider up and forward over the handle. |
| c | **Diving springboard** — the loaded board snaps up and launches you into a somersault | A loaded plank releasing under the body, but it is another plank and reads close to the seesaw being replaced. |

**Ranking.** (a) first: the trunk sits *across the underside of both thighs*, which is
literally where tori's bent leg goes, and a sapling whipping upright is a spring nobody
can misread. (b) second: the coil is the most explicit spring on offer and the handle
gives the rotation something to go over. (c) last: too near the rejected seesaw.

### 4a. `sapling` — TOP PICK

- **analogy_name:** `Launched by the bent sapling`
- **analogy:** `You climb onto a young tree that has been bent right down to the ground and sit astride the trunk. The tree has been waiting all afternoon to straighten out. It snaps up under your thighs and flings you up and forward off the top of it, heels over head.`
- **why_it_maps:** `The trunk pressed across the underside of your thighs is tori's coiled bent leg, and its sudden spring straight is the hane: a loaded spring under the legs that bounces the whole body up and over.`
- **image_prompt:**

```
Side view of a meadow, one frozen instant, whimsical cartoon ink drawing: a slender
young tree that had been bent down in a deep curve is caught in the act of springing
UPWARD and straightening, its trunk whipping up away from the ground with leaves
streaming behind it in that same upward direction; a laughing child who was sitting
astride that trunk, the trunk pressed across the underside of both thighs, has been
flung UP and FORWARD off it and is already a body's length above the trunk, legs still
open around the empty air where the trunk was, tipping into a forward somersault with
both sneakers rising over their head; the base of the tree where it meets the ground

continues up and forward from the top of the whipping trunk toward a soft haystack
```

## 7. Ushiro Goshi — *firm replacement* (`data/group-5.json`)

**Mechanic in one line:** lifted straight **up** off the floor from behind on a platform,
then the platform is taken away, so the fall is pure gravity onto the flat of the back.

**Candidates**

| # | Concept | Why it maps (one line) |
|---|---|---|
| a | **Flipped out of the pan** — the pancake is tossed straight up, the pan swings aside, and it comes down flat | Lifted vertically by a platform which then withdraws, leaving a flat landing and nothing else. |
| b | **Bounced off the hay wagon** — a pothole heaves you into the air and the wagon rolls on out from under you | The bed throws you straight up and then leaves, so you land flat on the road with nothing beneath. |
| c | **Car jack kicked away** — jacked up, then the jack is knocked out and the car slams flat | Mechanically exact, but there is no body and no peril a child can feel. |

**Ranking.** (a) first: it is the clearest possible picture of *lift, then remove the
platform, then land flat*, and everyone already knows the punchline. (b) second: it has a
human in it and the daylight between the floating body and the departed wagon does the
explaining. (c) last: cold.

### 7a. `pancake-flip` — TOP PICK

- **analogy_name:** `Flipped out of the pan`
- **analogy:** `The pancake gets tossed straight up off the pan and hangs there with nothing underneath it. Then the pan swings away sideways. There is no clever landing available. It comes straight back down and slaps the floor flat on its back.`
- **why_it_maps:** `The pan is tori's hips: it lifts the whole load vertically off the ground and then leaves, so all that is left is the drop, flat onto the back.`
- **image_prompt:**

```
Side view of a kitchen, one frozen instant, whimsical cartoon ink drawing: a grinning
cook has just tossed a big pancake straight UPWARD off a heavy skillet and has already
swung that skillet AWAY sideways out from under it, the pan now clearly off to one
side with nothing but empty air where it used to be; the pancake floats flat and
perfectly horizontal at the top of its rise, high above the floorboards, with a
startled comic face drawn on it and absolutely nothing beneath it; the space between
the underside of the pancake and the floor below is wide and empty, so the only thing
```
