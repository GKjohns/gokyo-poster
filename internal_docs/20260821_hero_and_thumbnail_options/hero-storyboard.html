Gentle Way · top-of-page options

Seven frames. The desktop frame is the top 900px of the real 1280px page, shown at 70%, with the real Dai Ikkyo group header and first row of cards so each header's weight reads against the grid. The 390px mobile frame is 1:1 and shows the header plus the first group header only. Palette and type stacks are the site's own; the mark is inlined from favicon.svg.

Every alternative sets all copy above the grid in the display serif. The current page is the only one with a third (sans) face up there. "Type sizes" counts distinct font sizes above the first group header at desktop width.

Option 1

Header bar only

A 64px bar is the whole top of the page: 32px mark and wordmark as the H1 on the left, the one-sentence description quietly on the right. The grid starts at about 120px. Nothing else.

  • 2 type sizes above the grid (22 wordmark, 15 sentence). H1: "Gentle Way", Latin. Kanji first appears in the group header, where it names something.
  • Gains: the poster is the page; highest grid start of any option; the bar is the same component the throw pages would use.
  • Loses: 五教の技 / Gokyo no Waza is no longer announced; it lives only in the bar sentence, the title tag and the footer. The least "poster" of the set.

Option 2

Header bar + compact title row

Mark-only bar with a quiet count on the right. One title row under it: "Gentle Way" at 56px left, and on the same baseline to the right the poster's name and description in one muted line. Then the grid.

  • 2 type sizes above the grid (56 H1, 15 for everything else). H1: "Gentle Way", Latin. Kanji only in the group headers.
  • Gains: a real, large wordmark aligned to the grid's left edge; "Gokyo no Waza" survives in the subtitle without a second heading size; grid starts near 250px.
  • Loses: the row is a wide-format idea, on mobile it stacks into a conventional title and subtitle; the bar mark and the H1 both say "brand", so the bar does little.

Option 3

Bold lockup, centered

The mark at 88px over a 72px "Gentle Way", one muted line, generous air, then the grid. The brand is the hero; the poster's name is the subtitle.

  • 2 type sizes above the grid (72 H1, 17 subtitle). H1: "Gentle Way", Latin. No kanji above the grid at all.
  • Gains: the mark finally has presence; one focal point; still reads as a poster sheet with a title.

Option 4

Poster title first, consolidated

Keep the poster's subject as the title, but as one lockup: 五教の技 and "Gokyo no Waza" on one line at matched cap height, left-aligned under a proper brand bar. One subtitle line. The hover hint moves to the right edge of the first group header. Eyebrow, seal dot and hint stack are gone.

  • 3 type sizes above the grid (20 bar, 56 title with the kanji optically matched at 46, 15 subtitle and hint). H1: "Gokyo no Waza" with kanji alongside.

  • Loses: still the busiest of the alternatives; the mixed-script title has to stack on mobile; the hint at the grid edge competes with the group name.

Option 5

Editorial masthead

A newspaper masthead: ruled bar with brand left and 柔道 right, a rule, then a two-column intro: H1 "Gokyo no Waza" in the left column, a two-sentence standfirst in the right that says what this is and how to use it. Then the grid.

  • 2 type sizes above the grid (18 for bar and standfirst, 52 H1). H1: "Gokyo no Waza", Latin; kanji appears once, small, in the masthead.
  • Gains: tells a first-time visitor what this is and how to use it in one read; the rules give the page a printed-sheet structure the card hairlines continue; the hover hint gets a proper sentence instead of a caption.
  • Loses: the most words of the set, and a standfirst is copy you have to keep true; the masthead mark is smaller than in 3 or 4.

Option 6

Chapters: the group header is the title

The bar carries the brand H1 and the one-line description; there is no title block. Each kyo header is promoted to a 40px chapter heading with a one-line lead, so 第一教 Dai Ikkyo is the first big thing on the page and the poster reads as five chapters of eight throws.

  • 3 type sizes above the grid (22 bar, 40 chapter, 15 lead). H1: "Gentle Way", Latin; the H2s carry kanji and romaji at equal size, the only place both scripts sit together.
  • Gains: the first heading is about the content, not the brand or the poster; kanji stays prominent as structure rather than ornament; the treatment repeats down the page, so the rhythm holds past the fold.
  • Loses: "Gokyo no Waza" exists only in the bar sentence; the chapter headings must keep their size on mobile or the idea collapses into option 1.

Reading the set

Options 1, 2, 3 and 6 make "Gentle Way" the H1 and push the poster's name down to one muted line; 4 and 5 keep "Gokyo no Waza" as the H1 and treat the brand as a bar. Every option drops the eyebrow, the seal dot and the uppercase hint as separate elements; where a hint survives it is a sentence in the same size as the subtitle.

If the page should be the poster and nothing else: 1 or 6. If it should still introduce itself: 2 (shortest) or 5 (most helpful). 3 is the brand-forward version. 4 is the closest to today with the clutter removed.
